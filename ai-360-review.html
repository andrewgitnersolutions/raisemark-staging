The Core Governance Question

“What AI tools are currently active inside our school district, who is using them, and where is our student data exposed?”

Before a district can adopt an enforceable Acceptable Use Policy or evaluate expensive AI vendor contracts, leadership must establish baseline visibility. In typical district environments, central IT oversees fewer than half of the AI tools actively accessed in classrooms. The remainder operates in the background — ungoverned browser extensions, personal teacher accounts, consumer chatbots, and mobile utilities that bypass procurement reviews.

The AI 360 Review (our Compass stage) replaces guesswork with empirical inventory. We do not sell software, and we do not grade districts with punitive labels. We give superintendents, CTOs, and boards the clear signal needed to govern with confidence.

Proprietary Methodology

How Does the CUEI-AI Framework Evaluate Student Privacy, Vendor Risk, and Educational Efficacy?

School districts rarely adopt AI in a single category. Instead, classrooms and central offices run dozens of disparate applications simultaneously — from student-facing writing assistants and adaptive math tutors to administrative workflow utilities and automated meeting summarizers.

Our proprietary CUEI-AI framework provides a standardized evaluation methodology across vastly different tools, allowing leadership to systematically determine their specific risk surfaces, operational drawbacks, and potential educational benefits. Rather than leaving leadership with abstract ratings, this analysis translates directly into four concrete administrative determinations:

RETAIN

High efficacy and vetted privacy compliance. Maintain enterprise contract and support continued adoption.

RIGHT-SIZE

Valuable capability with poor license usage. Renegotiate seat counts or consolidate redundant contracts.

SUNSET

Low usage rates, redundant capabilities, or unfavorable renewal terms. Structured phase-out with minimal classroom disruption.

DISCOURAGE

Unacceptable student privacy risks, model training exposure, or harmful pedagogical profile. Block at network edge.
